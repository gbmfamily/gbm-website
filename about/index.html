We believe that grace compels us to serve — in the church, in the community, and in every sphere of life.

Our History

The history of the founding of Grace Baptist Mission cannot be complete without reference to Ebenezer Baptist Church, Osogbo. The majority of the pioneering members of Grace Bible Mission evolved from Ebenezer Baptist Church. Grace Bible Mission formerly known as Grace Baptist Church, with her headquarters now located at No 1, Grace Bible Mission Street, Oke-Oniti, Osogbo, was inaugurated on April 12, 1987. The birth of the Church was not pre-planned but borne out of a leadership crisis between Ebenezer Baptist Church and Union Baptist Church, Osogbo (the mother church). The crisis began with the visit of a three-man delegation, led by Rev. T.F. Jemiriye, Pastor in-charge of Union Baptist Church, on March 9, 1986. The allegations levelled against the Executive Committee; Brother C. Ola Oni and Brother Julius O. Ogunkola (the then leaders), were unbaptistic worship practices, which they claimed, included constant vigils (Iso Oru), shouting of Hallelujah and praise God in the midst of worship services, praying aloud together, calling on non-Baptist ministers to lead revival services, and planting new churches (preaching stations) when she herself was still a preaching station. That was after 13 years of the church's existence. At that time, the Union Baptist Church indicated her intention to withdraw the then leaders of Ebenezer Baptist Church and also impose new leaders on the church. That move was however, strongly opposed. The church stressed that the alleged practices were and are rooted in fundamental principles and teachings of the Holy Bible. By then, Ebenezer Baptist Church's congregation had been transformed into a vibrant and militant praying people who could stand for God and claim their inheritances in Christ Jesus. On December 27, 1986, the Ebenezer Baptist Church Osogbo formally informed the mother church that her executive committee had set up a pulpit committee to scout for a pastor to man the church. In her response through a letter dated 29th Dec., 1986, the Union Baptist Church Osogbo disagreed to send a representative, and contended that it was the mother church's responsibility to constitute the search committee. It was further stated that leaders appointed at preaching stations should be changed every two years. It therefore named a four-man team to replace the existing leaders, with effect from the first Sunday in January, 1987. At that point in time, the leaders had served for over 13 years and the church had grown to a numerical strength of over 600 members. Through her letter dated 3rd January, 1987, Ebenezer Baptist Church, Osogbo, disagreed and rejected outrightly all the above moves by the Union Baptist Church. The church queried why the Union Baptist Church was just thinking of changing the leaders when the church was requesting for a trained licensed Baptist pastor. It rejected the idea of a change in leadership but opted for a trained licensed Baptist pastor to man the church. After the crisis was referred to and could not be resolved by Zion I Association, the Executive Committee of Ebenezer Baptist Church notified the Oyo East Conference of the situation. The appeal was met with rebuff by the chairman of the conference. Finally, the church notified the Nigerian Baptist Convention (NBC) of the situation through her secretary. The involvement of the Nigerian Baptist Convention took a new dimension when it directed the Oyo East Conference to install an interim pastor at Ebenezer Baptist Church. An induction service was held on March 1, 1987. Unfortunately, the interim pastor could not live up to the expected spirit of reconciliation and re-unification. The leadership of Union Baptist Church did not give him a free hand to operate. As a result of the prevailing circumstances, there was palpable tension in the church. Members of the Union Baptist Church and a few in Ebenezer Baptist Church were incited against some of the Ebenezer Baptist Church members. There were reports of public harassment and threats to life and property. From the pulpit, the interim pastor engaged in raining curses and making uncomplimentary remarks on the members of the church. At the climax of this persecution, seven brethren namely Brother C.O. Ola, Brother Julius O. Ogunlokola, Brother James O. Akinola, Brother J.I. Ibikunle, Brother Samson O. Ajayi, Brother Thompson O. Abiola and Brother J.A. Bamkole met together at the residence of Brother J.A. Bamkole on the 29th of March, 1987. They unanimously agreed that a new church should be founded instead of being entangled with continued hostility, bitterness and hatred. On April 12, 1987, during a church business meeting, the majority of the church officers each gave a report of stewardship and tendered letters of resignation. They remarked that there was no more love among members and that it was better to separate in peace rather than to continue in discord. Consequent upon this development, Grace Baptist Church (Fundamental, Independent), Osogbo was born in the evening of April 12, 1987, at No. 10, Olatunji Ajayi Street, Oke-Oniti Osogbo. The inaugural worship service was held at 5.00 pm, the same day, to mark the birth of Grace Baptist Church of Nigeria. Brother Julius O. Ogunlokola, who is now the General Overseer, officiated. Two hundred and fifty four people were in attendance. The membership continued to grow amazingly. Two acres of land were acquired for the development of her permanent site on 21st January 1990 at a cost of twenty five thousand naira only. It is situated at No. 1, Grace Bible Mission Street, Oke-Oniti opposite Railway Crossing, Osogbo. The foundation of Grace Baptist Church headquarters auditorium was laid on Saturday, June 24, 1989. To the glory of God the two thousand five hundred capacity auditorium was completed within nine months — a product of faith. At the commencement of work, the church had the sum of two thousand naira only in her bank account. The church went ahead in faith and she was not put to shame. After the exodus from Ebenezer Baptist Church, Beulah Baptist Church, Alekewodo, Osogbo voted to join Grace Baptist Church on her own volition. Beulah Baptist Church then became Grace Baptist Church, Alekewodo, Osogbo, under the leadership of Brothers C.O. Ola and J.I. Ibikunle. On 27th August 1992, the Federal Ministry of Internal Affairs finally gave its approval for the church's registration. The church gives glory to God and registers her appreciation for the roles played by all parties involved in achieving this feat.
